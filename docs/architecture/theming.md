# Theming

This document covers how an event gets its look: the colour palette and semantic roles, light and dark sets, the theme map and fonts, and the logos per surface.

## Colours, assets and the event stylesheet

Theming: each event's `config.php` has a `colors` map (e.g. `base`, `darker`, `primary`, `text`, `text-invert`, and the optional `nav-active`) and an `assets` map (logo/favicon paths under `www/events/<slug>/`). `View\TwigFactory` exposes the whole event config as the `event` Twig global; `_layout.twig` emits the colors as `--color-*` CSS custom properties in an inline `<style>` block, and `www/style.css` consumes them (`var(--color-base)`, etc.) — colour lives in the config, never in a stylesheet. An event may add one optional stylesheet for its non-colour look (shapes, type) as `assets.stylesheet`, a path under `www/`: `_layout.twig` links it through `asset_version()` right after `style.css`, `Precache` lists its versioned URL, and `ThemingTest` enforces that it names no colour literal, reads only `var(--role-…)`/`var(--theme-…)` with a fallback, and that every role an event declares is read by `style.css` or by that sheet. A rule there must never alter another event, and it must not give `<main>` a stacking context. `EventConfig::REQUIRED_COLORS` is checked at boot, so a half-themed event fails to start rather than rendering with unset properties. `www/style.css` must never name a colour itself; `ThemingTest::testCoreStylesheetHasNoColorLiterals` enforces that. Type is a seven-step `rem` scale in `:root` (`--fs-2xs` 11 … `--fs-2xl` 24 at a 16px root); two kinds of rule stay in px and `ThemingTest` names them: the chrome glyphs of the fixed-height bars and of the instrument controls (the timeline's zoom pair, the plan's `.plan-zoom-btn` floating on it), whose boxes are measured in pixels — `www/programs.js` reads the bars' heights as plain pixel numbers — and the timeline grid's type (`.tl-tick`, `.tl-now-label`, `.tl-stage`, `.tl-card`), whose geometry is frozen. The focus ring is one global two-tone `:focus-visible` rule in the ground pair (`--on-ground` outline, `--ground` halo); `.tabs-tab`, `.tl-card`, `.sheet-close` and `.day-chip` (its strip scrolls sideways, so it clips) move it inside their clipping box; the sheet's dialog container and the screen section (programmatic focus targets) draw none.

## The theme map and fonts

Next to `colors` sits an optional `theme` map (`EventConfig::$theme`, always an array) for the look-and-feel that is not colour — `font`, `font-display`, `font-display-weight`, `radius`/`radius-pill`/`radius-sheet`/`radius-card`, and `motion`. It is emitted as `--theme-*` in the same inline `<style>` block. There is no `font-url` any more: **every font and the icon font are self-hosted**. The `@font-face` rules for themix (400, 700), skautbold, Montserrat (400 to 900; latin and latin-ext, Google's v31 variable files with Google's `unicode-range`, so 800 is a real weight), and navigamus27's Seatren and Luciao Sans (one regular weight each, licensed for the event) live at the top of `www/style.css`, one woff2 `src` each, the files under `www/fonts/<family>/`; Font Awesome Free 5.8.1 is `www/vendor/fontawesome-free-5.8.1/` (its `all.min.css` byte-for-byte with its licence header, the three `.woff2` files it names, `LICENSE.txt`), linked by `_layout.twig` as `/vendor/fontawesome-free-5.8.1/css/all.min.css`. So `theme.font` may name only a family `www/style.css` declares (or a system font). `EventConfig::fontFamilies()` is what the event renders with — the first family of `theme.font` and `theme.font-display`, quotes stripped, or `themix`/`skautbold` where unset, plus `Font Awesome` always — and `App\Http\Fonts` is the plain table family → web paths that `Precache` puts into the offline set; `Tests\Unit\FontsTest` keeps that table, the `@font-face` rules and Font Awesome's own `url()`s in step.

## Semantic roles

Colour is **semantic roles**, not palette keys. No rule in `www/style.css` names a `--color-*` any more; every one reads a role, and the role definitions in `:root` are the only place the palette is touched (`ThemingTest::testNoRuleInTheStylesheetNamesAPaletteKey` enforces exactly that). The rule that keeps the system honest is **one colour, one role** — if an event's green is `state`, green is not also a ground, a surface or a piece of chrome. The set is sized for a 60:30:10 budget: `ground`/`on-ground` with `surface`/`on-surface` as its one block tier (60), `structure`/`on-structure` for the bars, the sheet, the buttons, the toast and the timeline's cards (30), and `action`/`on-action`/`action-link` plus `tonal`/`on-tonal` and `state`/`on-state` sharing the 10, with `edge`, `edge-width`, `heading`, `emphasis` and `scrim` in support, and `now` — the timeline's current-time line and its label, under `on-action` ink — falling back to `action`. `structure` defaults to `--color-darker` rather than `--color-base`: it is the one role that is a filled ground under inverted type at body size, and obrok19's base carries `--color-text-invert` at 3.85:1 where its darker green reaches 6.84:1. Three scopes **rebind** the core roles rather than adding names: a text field, `.sheet-card` and `.tl-scroll` are each their own little surface with their own structure on it, so the grid's cards are simply `structure` in the grid's scope. The rebinding sits on `.tl-scroll` and not on `.pg` because custom properties inherit — on `.pg` it would also reach the day strips and the view tabs.

## Light and dark

**Light mode is light and dark mode is dark, all the way through.** `structure` grounds every bar, sheet and card, so in a light set it is the palest thing on the screen and in a dark set the darkest; a filled near-black app bar in light mode is the "black element in the light mode" the system exists to prevent, and a lifted mid-grey card at night is the same mistake upside down (`ThemingTest::testTheLargeFilledAreasFollowTheMode` measures the relative luminance of every role that names a large fill and holds each mode to its own end of the ramp). Two things follow. First, an accent can only be **ink or a small area filled under dark ink**, never a large fill: obrok27's `#e0ff00` is 1.01:1 on its pale ground and obrok19's `#2a9272` is 3.49:1, so the **active tab is filled with the event's colour and carries `on-state`** (15.4:1 and 4.8:1 under near-black ink) rather than tinted type — the fill is the whole tab, not a chip behind the glyph, because tinted type at those ratios is the regression this rule exists to prevent — and `state` has one shape in both modes; the **app bar, the current day chip (`.day-chip[aria-current="true"]`, under `on-state` like the active tab) and the `Přihlášen` tag (`.pl-tag`, a small fill carrying the ground's hairline) are the only other surfaces filled with `state`**, and everything on the app bar — the title and the profile link with its avatar — therefore takes `on-state` too, because that fill does not flip with the mode while `on-structure` does (it used to take `on-structure`, which put obrok19's body grey on green at 2.56:1); a secondary `.btn`, a `.highlight` link CTA and a `.notice` are **`tonal`** — the accent washed almost all the way into the ground, carrying the accent itself at full strength as ink. `.btn-primary` is unchanged in kind, a filled accent under inverted ink. Second, separation cannot come from a tone step alone, because content scrolls under a bar of very nearly its own colour, so **every bar, sheet and card also carries a hairline, in both modes** — `--edge-width`, still 0 for an event that declares nothing. Light hairlines are neutral and dark ones keep the accent tint; each is meant to clear 3:1 against both sides of itself, which is what the 1px content-block outlines replaced the old 4px frames for (obrok27's dark `edge` does not yet, about 1.8:1 on its ground, until its new palette).

## The identity role

One role sits beside `structure` rather than inside it: `signature`/`on-signature`, **structure that carries the identity**. It behaves like structure — a filled band of chrome with type on it — but its value is a quiet cast of the event's own colour, and the **hour ruler is its only use in the app**; painting a second surface with it would return the screen to the state where nothing could read as an accent (`ThemingTest::testTheIdentityRoleIsUsedByTheHourRulerAndNothingElse` holds it to one). It is deliberately *not* the accent at full strength: a saturated slab is the loudest thing on a light screen and a lit panel at night, and the ruler is chrome. The stage column has its own pair (`stage`/`on-stage`) in the grid's scope because the card and the column want different tones out of the same tier — the card separates from the grid by a step plus its hairline, the column is a band behind bold names — but its value is a **plain neutral in both modes**, because a hue on the largest block of the screen is how accents stop reading. Both pairs fall back to the grid's own `structure`, so an event that declares neither renders exactly as it did. The registered card has a pair of its own in the same scope: `registered-bg` (with `registered-line`/`registered-width`) and its ink **`on-registered`** (`--reg-fg`, falling back to `on-structure`), because on an accent fill the grid's card ink can fall far below AA (navigamus27's light set was 2.0:1); every kissj-design event names it in both sets. korbo26's dark set, likewise, carries a lighter orange (`#f08a3c`) as its `action-link`, `heading` and `emphasis` ink, its fill colour `#dd6700` being 4.39:1 on the dark surface.

## Fallbacks and the hand-authored sets

Every role falls back to exactly the expression its rules used before roles existed, and those **built-in fallbacks are frozen** — they are the pre-round-10 look, dark chrome and all, and the fixture events under `tests/fixtures/events/` are what still exercise them (`testEveryRoleFallsBackToThePalette`, `testStructureDefaultsToThePalettesDeeperTone`). No shipped event uses them: **obrok19 hand-authors a `light` set of its own** and is no longer the frozen control — its tab bar is white, its green fills the app bar, the active tab and the registered programmes, its red is `action`, and `docs/baseline/obrok19/` is re-taken by `bin/baseline.php` at the end of every round, so a diff against it is still how a round shows it disturbed nothing. obrok19 stays **light-only**: no `dark` set, no mode control. An event that wants its own colours declares `roles` as two **hand-authored** sets, `light` and `dark` (`EventConfig::$roles`) — accents do not survive mechanical inversion, so changing a colour is deliberately a two-place edit. `_layout.twig` emits light as the base, then the dark set twice: once under `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` scoped to `:root:not([data-mode="light"])` so the device's setting works without JS, and once under `:root[data-mode="dark"]` so an explicit choice wins. A tiny **inline script in `<head>`**, before the stylesheet link, sets `data-mode` from `localStorage['obrokColorMode']` (global, not per event) or from `prefers-color-scheme` — never the deferred script at the foot of the page, or the reader sees a frame of the wrong theme. The choice is made on **Profil**, logged in or not (the look is the browser's, not the participant's), and only for an event that declares a dark set: a three-way `<fieldset class="mode-control" data-mode-control>` (`Vzhled`: radios `Světlý`, `Tmavý`, `Podle systému`, one `name="mode"`), handled in `www/app.js` by a delegated `change` listener — `Podle systému` removes the stored choice so the device setting rules again — and re-checked from storage on `DOMContentLoaded`, `screen:shown` and `screen:morphed`. The app bar carries no mode toggle.

## Logos per surface

Colour reaches the **assets** too, because a bar that follows the mode cannot carry one fixed drawing. `assets.menuLogo` is the tab-bar mark and `menuLogoDark` its dark-mode twin (`.mode-light-only`/`.mode-dark-only` swap them), while `menuLogoOnState` is the drawing for a surface filled with `state` — that fill is the event's own colour, so a mark in that same colour disappears on it. Which drawing goes where follows from what the surface is filled with, and **the tab bar and the app bar are not the same kind of surface**: the tab bar is `structure` and flips with the mode, so it takes the mode pair, but the app bar is `state` and does not, so it takes `menuLogoOnState` exactly as the active tab does. `assets.appBarLogo` overrides that for the app bar alone (precedence `appBarLogo`, `menuLogoOnState`, `menuLogo`); the tab bar never reads it. obrok27 declares `appBarLogo` (the white lilie, on its dark bar in both modes), `mainLogo`/`mainLogoDark` (the wordmark) and no `menuLogo`, so the home tab shows the `fa-home` icon; its `bar`/`tabbar` roles fill the two bars and are read only by `events/obrok27/style.css`, so both bars stay dark in light mode. navigamus27 does the same with its own `bar`/`on-bar` (kissj's purple band, `#1b0e44` light and `#2b1977` dark, its wavy bottom edge cut inside the bar's box): `appBarLogo` is the N27 mark with white text, `mainLogo`/`mainLogoDark` the full logo (the light one on a white card), and no `menuLogo`. Each asset is optional and the classes are only emitted when the second drawing exists, so an event with one logo keeps showing it everywhere. `mainLogo`/`mainLogoDark` do the same job on Domů.
